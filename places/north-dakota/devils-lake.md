## Geography and climate risks

- Devils Lake sits in a closed basin in the Red River watershed and has not reached its natural spill point to the Sheyenne River in recorded history. [1]
- The lake hit its recorded low in 1940 (1,401 ft) and rose through the 1990s and 2000s. [1][2]

## Events and incidents

- June 2011: the lake reached its modern high of about 1,454.3 ft; state and federal highways near the city had been raised and some roads closed, with flood damage to private property. [1][2]
- Outlets: the state built its own outlet (2003 or 2005, sources differ) and the Corps control structure dates to 2012; mitigation is reported to have cost North Dakota and the federal government more than $450 million. [2]
